Introduction

“If the past few years have taught us anything, it’s that we exist in a marketplace that’s truly global in nature. Supply chains stretch around the world and are increasingly capable of serving customers around the world” (Blue Yonder and Ernst & Young LLP 1). Companies have applied advanced technologies, like artificial intelligence, machine learning, and data science to uncover new revenue opportunities while innovating in their ability to ship and deliver to diverse locations, from homes to micro-fulfillment centers. These developments are built upon the operational expertise of freight forwarders and global logisticians, whose work in coordinating shipments, navigating trade regulations, and optimizing routes remains the foundation of every advanced supply chain solution (Fraser et al. 3).

Global supply chains are no longer linear systems. Every node, from component suppliers to end customers, interacts dynamically. Freight forwarders must balance cost, speed, and reliability while logisticians assess labor, capacity, and geopolitical risks. These core operational decisions feed into AI-driven platforms like Blue Yonder’s Global Synchronous Execution, allowing organizations to make real-time adjustments across multiple countries while maintaining compliance and service levels (Blue Yonder and Ernst & Young LLP 6).
