Resilience & Risk

“Given that the disruption that caused these supply chain failures seems likely to become a more common feature of global business, an investment in building operational resilience looks increasingly vital” (Deloitte, “Resilient Operations”). Resilience involves more than redundancy; it requires anticipating disruptions, interpreting signals, and adjusting resources across the supply chain. Freight forwarders’ knowledge of multi-tier suppliers and routes allows organizations to detect vulnerabilities before they cascade into production or delivery failures (Fraser et al. 17).

Political interventions, labor shortages, and ESG considerations further complicate global logistics. Access to semiconductors is concentrated in a few sensitive regions, and ESG pressures require ethical sourcing and environmental compliance. Logisticians must adapt to these pressures while aligning inventory, transportation, and labor decisions with both operational objectives and social responsibility (Deloitte, “Resilient Operations”; Fraser et al. 20).
