Regulatory Bodies

Global trade relies on regulatory bodies to maintain compliance, safety, and fair practices. Agencies such as the World Trade Organization (WTO) oversee international trade rules, while the International Maritime Organization (IMO) governs shipping standards and safety. Customs and Border Protection (CBP) in the United States manages imports and exports, ensuring adherence to trade agreements and security protocols.

Other organizations include the European Union’s Directorate-General for Trade (DG TRADE), which negotiates trade agreements and monitors compliance within EU member states; the Food and Agriculture Organization (FAO), which regulates food standards and sustainability; and the International Air Transport Association (IATA), which manages global air cargo standards. These bodies shape operational decisions, influence shipping routes, and enforce rules that logisticians and freight forwarders must integrate into daily execution.
